Introduction:

The rapid growth of online activity has increased the need for users to recognize and avoid potentially fraudulent or malicious websites. Fake websites can imitate legitimate brands, collect sensitive information, redirect users to harmful pages, or create financial and security risks. As online interactions become increasingly common, understanding how people identify suspicious websites and what type of support they need can help inform the development of more effective website-safety solutions.

This analysis presents findings from a survey of 163 respondents conducted to understand users’ online browsing habits, experiences with potentially fake or malicious websites, confidence in identifying unsafe websites, and expectations for an AI-powered website detection platform.

The analysis is presented through the Safe Link AI Analytics Dashboard, an interactive dashboard designed to examine user behaviour, awareness, confidence, preferences, and perceived requirements for an AI-powered website safety solution.

The dashboard focuses on two closely connected perspectives:

User Online Behaviour and Website-Safety Awareness
User Expectations and Requirements for SafeLink AI

The objective is to transform the survey responses into meaningful insights that explain how frequently users interact with online links, how confident they are when assessing website safety, what warning or explanation features they prefer, and how willing they are to use an AI-powered website detection platform.

The analysis therefore goes beyond presenting survey percentages. It seeks to answer a broader question:

How prepared are users to identify potentially unsafe websites, and what kind of AI-powered assistance would make website safety decisions easier and more reliable?

10. In-Analysis

10.1 Prior Experience With Fake Websites

One of the most significant findings in the dashboard is the proportion of respondents who have previously encountered a fake website.

The dashboard records:

*76.1% — Prior Fake Website Encounter*

This means that more than three-quarters of the surveyed respondents reported having encountered a fake website previously.

Key Insight: Exposure to Fake Websites Is Widespread

The result demonstrates that fake websites are not simply an abstract cybersecurity concern for the respondents. A large majority have already experienced some form of exposure.

This strengthens the case for tools that can help users evaluate website safety before they interact with potentially harmful pages.

10.2 Average Link-Clicking Behaviour

The dashboard records an overall average of:

*7.23 links clicked per day*

This provides an indication of the level of online interaction among respondents.

Key Insight: Frequent Link Interaction Creates Safety Exposure

Users who regularly click links may encounter a wide variety of websites, including legitimate, suspicious, or potentially malicious pages.

Therefore, website detection tools can provide value by supporting users at the point where they need to make a safety decision.

10.3 Online Activities

The dashboard examines respondents’ online activities alongside their average link-clicking behaviour.

The activities represented include areas such as:

* Social media
* Online banking
* School or academic research
* Streaming
* Online shopping
* Remote work
* Government services
* Other online activities

Social media represents the largest respondent share in the dashboard at approximately **92.0%**, while other activities also demonstrate substantial levels of participation.

### Key Insight: Website Safety Applies Across Multiple Online Activities

Users do not interact with websites for only one purpose. Social communication, financial activity, education, shopping, entertainment, and work can all involve links and website interactions.

This means that an effective website detection solution should be useful across multiple online contexts rather than being designed for a single type of user activity.

10.4 Confidence in Spotting Fake Websites

The dashboard records an overall confidence score of:

*3.58 — Confidence in Spotting a Fake Website*

The second dashboard also breaks down respondents according to their confidence in website verification, including neutral, confident, and less-confident response categories.

Key Insight: User Confidence Does Not Eliminate the Need for Assistance

Although some respondents demonstrate confidence in identifying suspicious websites, the overall results suggest that users can still benefit from an additional verification layer.

An AI-powered detection system could therefore act as a support mechanism, helping users make more informed decisions rather than requiring them to depend entirely on personal judgement.


10.5 Experience With Malicious Websites

The dashboard examines respondents’ experience with malicious websites.

The visualization shows a strong representation of respondents who reported having experienced a malicious website.

Key Insight: Malicious Website Exposure Is a Practical Concern

The presence of previous malicious-website experiences reinforces the need for preventive website-safety measures.

A detection platform can potentially reduce risk by identifying suspicious characteristics and communicating them to users before they proceed.

10.6 Willingness to Use SafeLink AI

The second dashboard presents an important user-adoption indicator:

*83.4% — Willingness to Use SafeLink*

This represents a strong level of interest among respondents in using an AI-powered website detection solution.

Key Insight: Strong Potential for User Adoption

The high willingness-to-use percentage suggests that respondents recognize the potential value of an AI-assisted website safety solution.

This is particularly important because technology adoption depends not only on whether a solution exists but also on whether users see enough value in using it.

10.7 Importance of Risk Explanation

The dashboard records:

*81.0% — Risk Explanation Importance*

This indicates that respondents place considerable importance on understanding why a website may be considered risky.

Key Insight: Users Want Explanations, Not Just Warnings

A website detection system should therefore avoid providing only a simple *safe/unsafe* classification.

Users may benefit more when the system explains the factors contributing to the risk assessment. This can improve transparency and help users develop stronger website-safety awareness.

10.8 Preferred Result Format

The dashboard examines how respondents would prefer SafeLink AI to communicate its detection results.

The leading preferences include:

*Safe/Unsafe — 57 respondents*
*A combination — 56 respondents*
*Risk score — 29 respondents*
*Numerical risk information — 21 respondents*

Key Insight: Simple Results Should Be Combined With Useful Context

The strong preference for the Safe/Unsafe option shows that users value straightforward conclusions.

At the same time, the popularity of the combination option suggests that users may also appreciate additional information alongside the final classification.

A practical solution could therefore provide a clear safety status while also presenting a concise risk explanation.

10.9 Most Wanted SafeLink AI Features

The dashboard identifies several features respondents would like to see in the platform.

These include:

*Scan history — 49.6%*
*Personalized security — 45.0%*
*Scam reporting — 41.7%*
*Browser extension — 39.2%*
*Email phishing scan — 36.2%*
*Community reporting — 34.9%*
*QR code scanner — 26.9%*

Key Insight: Users Want a Broader Safety Ecosystem

The results show that respondents are interested in more than basic website classification.

Features such as scan history, personalized security, reporting mechanisms, browser integration, phishing detection, community reporting, and QR-code scanning indicate demand for a more comprehensive website-safety solution.


10.10 Website-Safety Checking Behaviour

The dashboard examines the methods respondents currently use when deciding whether a website is safe.

The leading behaviours include:

* Checking the website
* Checking the brand
* Relying on browser warnings
* Checking online reviews

Other respondents reported behaviours such as clicking links directly or using fewer verification methods.

Key Insight: Users Already Apply Several Safety Checks

The findings show that respondents are not completely dependent on a single safety method.

However, manually checking websites, brands, reviews, and browser warnings may not always provide users with a complete understanding of the risk.

An AI-powered solution could bring several indicators together and provide a more centralized assessment.

Key Insight: Users Depend on Multiple Existing Tools

10.12 Risk Explanation and User Expectations

The dashboard’s risk-explanation indicator shows:

Key Insight: Transparency Is Central to the User Experience

The strong importance placed on risk explanation indicates that users want to understand the reasoning behind a safety decision.

For an AI-powered platform, this means the user experience should emphasize *clarity, transparency, and understandable risk factors*, rather than presenting an unexplained AI decision.

12. Strategic Interpretation

The dashboard presents a clear picture of the relationship between online activity, website-safety awareness, and demand for AI-powered assistance.

A large proportion of respondents have already encountered fake websites, while users also report frequent internet usage and regular link-clicking behaviour. At the same time, the strong willingness to use SafeLink AI demonstrates that respondents see potential value in an automated website-safety solution.

However, the findings also show that *detection alone may not be sufficient*.

Users want to know whether a website is safe, but they also want to understand why a website has been classified as risky. This makes explainability an important part of the proposed platform.

The analysis therefore suggests that an effective SafeLink AI solution should combine:

* Website detection
* Clear safety classification
* Risk explanation
* Personalized security
* Scam reporting
* Phishing detection
* Browser integration
* Scan history
* QR-code scanning
* Community reporting

Actionable Recommendations

1 Prioritize Explainable AI

Because risk explanation is highly valued, SafeLink AI should provide understandable reasons behind its website classifications.

2 Provide Simple but Informative Results

The platform should clearly communicate whether a website is safe or unsafe while also offering a concise explanation and, where appropriate, a risk score.

3 Develop the Most Requested Features

Development priorities should include scan history, personalized security, scam reporting, browser-extension functionality, and phishing detection.

4 Support Multiple Online Environments

Because respondents use the internet for social media, banking, education, shopping, work, and other activities, SafeLink AI should be designed for broad online usage.

5 Improve User Awareness

The platform can incorporate educational explanations that help users recognize common warning signs rather than making them completely dependent on automated detection.

6 Consider Browser and QR-Code Integration

The demand for browser extensions and QR-code scanning suggests that SafeLink AI could extend beyond a standalone website and support users in the environments where suspicious links are commonly encountered.

7 Encourage Community Reporting

A reporting system could allow users to contribute information about suspicious websites, potentially strengthening the platform’s ability to identify emerging threats.

 Conclusion

The SafeLink AI-Powered Fake Website Detection Platform represents an innovative response to one of today’s significant cybersecurity challenges.

Through systematic data collection, structured data preparation, and comprehensive analysis, the project established an evidence-based foundation for understanding users’ online safety behaviours, experiences with fake and malicious websites, and expectations of an AI-powered website detection solution.

The findings indicate that internet users are interested in practical cybersecurity solutions that combine effective threat detection with clear explanations and intuitive user experiences. By incorporating these insights into product development, SafeLink AI has the potential to improve online safety, strengthen user confidence, and contribute to broader efforts aimed at addressing phishing attacks, fraudulent websites, and other online threats.

Ultimately, the effectiveness of SafeLink AI will depend not only on the accuracy of its detection capabilities but also on its ability to provide **trustworthy, transparent, accessible, and user-centred cybersecurity assistance. By empowering users with understandable risk information and practical safety features, the platform can support individuals in making safer and more informed decisions while browsing the internet.
